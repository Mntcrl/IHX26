# App Attest in Galaxy v1

Galaxy verifies Apple App Attest locally. Enrollment requires Apple's App Attest service on a supported device; a development-signed build obtains development-environment keys (observed on our test device with a free-team Debug build, see below), while production-environment keys require the App Attest entitlement set to `production` in a distribution-signed build. There is no Galaxy challenge server or Apple service call during verification. Apple and the pinned trust anchor remain dependencies for provenance; XRPL provides authenticated account transactions, ledger order and the public registration and capture challenges. The Groth16 proof, not App Attest, establishes the stated physical-consistency property of the committed inputs.

## Registration and publication

1. Generate an App Attest key. Form the registration `clientDataHash` per [§3](galaxy-v1-spec.md#3-sha-256-identifiers-domain-tag--ascii-bytes-followed-by-0x00) with the validated ledger index/hash, a fresh 32-byte random nonce, the Apple key ID, the XRPL account ID and the network ID. Give the 32-byte hash to `DCAppAttestService.attestKey`.
2. Strip `attStmt.receipt` before broadcasting the DEVICE bundle. `strip_receipt` emits deterministic CBOR with the original `x5c` certificates and `authData` bytes. Chunk the bundle and send the chunks and DEVICE manifest from that account. The signed certificate binds the nonce to the authenticator data; the server-side receipt is not necessary to check that binding. Attestation verification can also consume an object with a receipt, but never interprets or retains that field.
3. The ledger verifier checks the validated manifest and chunk transactions, their account, the reconstructed bundle ID and length, the registration ledger hash, and the `reg_ledger_index ..= reg_ledger_index + 100` window. `verify_registration` then checks that the manifest's overlapping fields equal the bundle's, and verifies at the DEVICE manifest ledger close time supplied as **Unix seconds** (not wall-clock time or XRPL-epoch seconds).
4. Decode bounded CBOR with exactly the expected fields; require `fmt = "apple-appattest"` and exactly two `x5c` entries, credential first. Validate the leaf and intermediate profiles, names, times and signatures against the [Apple App Attestation Root CA](https://www.apple.com/certificateauthority/Apple_App_Attestation_Root_CA.pem). The pin is SHA-256 of the root DER, `1cb9823ba28ba6ad2d33a006941de2ae4f513ef1d4e831b9f7e0fa7b6242c932`. The root and intermediate use P-384; the root signs the intermediate with ECDSA/SHA-384, and the intermediate signs the P-256 leaf with ECDSA/SHA-256. No system CA store, alternate root, AIA fetch, or online revocation lookup is used; Galaxy's ledger revocation is a separate check.
5. Compare the 32-byte nonce in credential certificate extension `1.2.840.113635.100.8.2` (DER `SEQUENCE { [1] EXPLICIT OCTET STRING }`) to `SHA256(authData ‖ clientDataHash)`. Require a zero sign counter, `rpIdHash = SHA256(app_id)`, and the development or production AAGUID matching the bundle and manifest. `key_id` must equal both the credential ID and `SHA256` of the leaf's 65-byte uncompressed X9.62 point, and the embedded EC2/ES256 COSE key must express that same point. Both the app ID and environment must be permitted by verifier policy.

Galaxy is an iOS protocol; do not add macOS signing identifiers to the app allowlist as a substitute for implementing Apple's separate macOS ACL policy. App Attest development and production use distinct AAGUIDs and report distinct classes. Policy must opt in to each Apple environment; empty allowlists accept no Apple credentials.

## Capture assertion and replay

`clientData = "GALAXY/ASSERT/v1\0" ‖ core299`. Pass `SHA256(clientData)` to `generateAssertion`. The verifier checks account and network against the verified registration, extracts `signature` and `authenticatorData`, checks the RP ID hash and positive big-endian 32-bit sign counter, and forms `nonce = SHA256(authenticatorData ‖ SHA256(clientData))`.

Verify the **DER** ECDSA signature using ES256 over **the message `nonce`**; the ECDSA digest is `SHA256(nonce)`. Do not pass `nonce` directly to a prehashed ECDSA verifier and do not try either form as a fallback. [Apple's verification steps](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/devicecheck/validating-apps-that-connect-to-your-server) describe ES256 verification for `nonce`. [node-app-attest](https://github.com/uebelack/node-app-attest/blob/493d029e2d6a7e6bc8b4e4d032b01fb204b85c39/src/verifyAssertion.js#L80-L91) uses `createVerify("SHA256")` over `nonce`; [devicecheck-appattest](https://github.com/veehaitch/devicecheck-appattest/blob/cb26211f63c1e2e7949deafe2efdf352daca27fa/src/main/kotlin/ch/veehait/devicecheck/appattest/assertion/AssertionValidator.kt#L85-L104) does the same with `SHA256withECDSA`. An OpenSSL-backed test of node-app-attest's public device assertion verified `SHA256(nonce)` and rejected the raw-nonce digest. A checked-in regression verifies both cases against a second, Apple-root-validated, same-key device pair.

Confirmed on our own device: an iPhone 13 Pro Max (iPhone14,3, iOS 27.2.1) produced a development attestation for app ID `PPS57BF3X3.utcq.XRPL-Galaxy` over a Galaxy §3 DEVICE-REG `clientDataHash` and two assertions with counters 1 and 2. In `real_device_registration_and_assertions_share_key`, `verify_registration` checks the pinned Apple chain, nonce, RP ID hash, development AAGUID, key ID and zero counter. Both assertions verify with the key returned by that registration and the §3 assertion hash, but fail when `nonce` itself is used as the ECDSA digest. The probe signed 299-byte placeholders rather than valid CAPTURE cores; `verify_capture_assertion` rejects them at its wire-format check. These vectors demonstrate App Attest interoperability, not a published XRPL capture. The device assertions set AT (`0x40`) with no attested credential data and no extensions, matching the legacy layout below.

The authenticator byte layout is Apple's rather than the generic WebAuthn profile. Apple attestations have the AT bit and an AAGUID, credential ID and COSE key. Some older *assertions* set AT even though they contain no such fields. Apple's [current validation guide](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/devicecheck/attestation-object-validation-guide) appends an extensions map in its signed `authData` with ED clear. Galaxy accepts legacy extensionless records and, when present, parses exactly the two documented Apple fields (nonempty `apple_bundle_version_01` text and a valid little-endian `apple_validation_category_01` UInt32); arbitrary trailing data, unexpected flags, and malformed extensions fail closed. Galaxy v1 does not impose a bundle release allowlist. The guide's published nonce example matches `authData ‖` the **raw** `example_server_challenge`, not the SHA-256 challenge described in the prose. Galaxy does not adopt that example's input: its registration hash is always the 32-byte value in §3.

Call `verify_counter` on authenticated counters of the same registration, compared by `(ledger_index, TransactionIndex)`. Accept a counter only if it is strictly greater than every earlier one (not only the nearest); reject an already-seen position. Gapless counters are not required. The verifier supplies complete earlier CAPTURE history and independently enforces `capture_id` uniqueness and revocation. A signature check alone is not replay protection.

## Development and privacy

Dev-unattested credentials (`0xF0`) carry the raw Ed25519 public key and the key ID `SHA256("GALAXY/DEV-KEY/v1\0" ‖ public_key[32])`, and sign `"GALAXY/DEV-ASSERT/v1\0" ‖ core299`. They have no Apple sign counter or attested app/device identity. A valid dev credential and signature still report **UNSUPPORTED** (development unattested credential), never `VERIFIED`. An Apple development attestation is not the same class.

An attestation certifies a key for an Apple-recognized app/device and an assertion establishes use of that key for the signed bytes. It does not prove an uncompromised execution path or code identity at capture time, attest the sensor values, prevent a compromised device from supplying inputs, or establish scene truth. Galaxy's ledger challenges replace a private one-time server challenge by binding the data to a wallet, a validated ledger, and a nonce; ledger history supplies public replay checks. It retains none of Apple's receipt-based device risk assessment because it neither uses nor publishes receipts. Apple describes the receipt as input to a server-to-server fraud metric; it can contain additional device-correlating material. Key IDs, app IDs, ledger times, XRPL account IDs, and assertions are still public and link activity across captures; receipt stripping does not make captures anonymous.

## Credential certificate lifetime

Apple's credential certificates are short-lived. Our device's development leaf is valid for three days (2026-11-19 19:44:00 to 2026-11-22 19:44:00 UTC), as is the 2021 public development sample's (2021-01-22 to 2021-01-25); the 2024 public production sample's leaf runs from 2024-02-06 to 2024-12-21. Because Galaxy evaluates the chain at the DEVICE manifest ledger close time, the app must publish the bundle and manifest within the leaf's validity window, in practice immediately after `attestKey`; a later manifest fails with `certificate_time` and the app must attest a new key. Captures do not depend on the leaf lifetime: assertions are verified with the registered public key after a successful registration, so an old registration keeps verifying (subject to revocation). This differs from Apple's server model, where a server validates the attestation once on receipt and stores the key; publication on XRPL makes the manifest ledger time the evaluation instant.

## Verifier API and error codes

`verify_registration(bundle, manifest, manifest_ledger_close_unix_seconds, accepted) -> VerifiedCredential` (key ID, public key, environment, class `AppleDevelopment | AppleProduction | DevUnattested`, app ID, account) and `verify_capture_assertion(cred, core299, assertion) -> AssertionInfo { counter: Option<u32> }`; `verify_counter(candidate, history)` applies the counter policy; `strip_receipt` prepares the bundle attestation; `assertion_counter_hint` returns an unauthenticated counter for indexing only. Errors are `AttestError` variants with stable codes from `AttestError::code()`: `size`, `cbor`, `attestation_format`, `manifest_binding`, `capture_core`, `capture_binding`, `root_pin`, `certificate_encoding`, `certificate_time`, `certificate_profile`, `certificate_chain`, `nonce_encoding`, `nonce_mismatch`, `app_id`, `environment`, `key_id`, `public_key`, `authenticator_data`, `flags`, `extensions`, `counter`, `counter_replay`, `signature_encoding`, `signature`. `test-vectors/attestation/cases.json` lists 60 positive and negative cases with these codes for other implementations.
